The lighthouse keeper had not spoken to anyone in eleven days. Every morning, he climbed the hundred and twelve steps, wiped the salt from the glass, and wrote the weather in a small blue notebook. On the twelfth day, a boat appeared on the horizon. It was too small for a fishing trawler and too slow for the coast guard. He watched it for an hour, then went down to the dock with a lantern, although it was still light. The boat carried a girl of about ten and a very old dog. She said her grandfather had told her that the light was broken. The keeper laughed, for the first time in months, and told her that the light had never failed, not once in forty years. The girl looked at him carefully. Then she pointed at the tower. The lamp was dark. He had forgotten to turn it on the night before, and the night before that. They climbed the stairs together, the dog following slowly, and that evening the light swept across the water again.
