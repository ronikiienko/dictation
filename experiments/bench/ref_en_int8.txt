Yes, both run as int8 on the CPU. int8: the script loads the encoder.int8.onnx, decoder.int8.onnx and joiner.int8.onnx files for both models. CPU: both use provider="cpu" with 4 threads (the --threads option). The sherpa-onnx package from pip only supports the CPU anyway, so your GPU isn't used at all. "int8" here means dynamic quantization, which is the usual way ONNX speech models are converted. The weights of the large matrix multiplications are stored as int8. These make up most of the model, which is why the files are about 650 MB instead of about 2.4 GB. Activations (the values passed between layers) are converted to int8 on the fly for those multiplications. Some smaller operations, such as normalization and parts of the convolutions, may stay in fp32. So it's not every operation in 8-bit, but most of the computation is. That's the standard way these models run on the CPU, and the accuracy loss compared with fp32 is usually very small. We could download the fp32 versions to check that on your own recordings later, but they'd be about 2.4 GB each.
